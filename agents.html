Concept

Highlightext is a text-highlighting project intended to make important information easier to spot during research, analysis, and everyday work. Its brand combines black and electric yellow with the familiar visual metaphor of a highlighter.

The brand artwork states: “Spot key information instantly,” “Built for research & analysis,” “Privacy first, works locally,” and “AI-assisted highlights (optional).” These are brand concepts, not verified capabilities of a released product.

Current implementation and project status

The visual website includes a working browser-local keyword demonstration. Readers can choose Research, Contracts, or Campaigns, enter comma-separated terms, apply case-insensitive literal matches, and hide or show highlights. Example passages are illustrative. The demo does not upload entered terms or implement AI. The website loads Google Analytics to measure visits.

This repository contains project descriptions, brand artwork, a target-market research file, keyword settings, profile exports, and a master-data builder. It does not currently include a released extension, an installation package, or an AI highlighting service.

This alternative page is plain semantic HTML with no visual CSS, images, or external fonts. It includes the site-wide Google Analytics tag. All information is available directly in the source.

Campaign management for political candidates

Proposed ways Highlightext can help campaign teams review public information and community discussions.

  1. Issue Sentiment Monitoring

    Highlight positive, negative, and neutral language around issues such as housing, taxes, schools, and public safety. Help researchers collect comments for sentiment analysis.

  2. Constituent Concern Discovery

    Identify recurring complaints, questions, and concerns in local news, forums, and public community discussions.

  3. Community Stakeholder Discovery

    Find publicly identified neighborhood leaders, nonprofit organizations, advocacy groups, business associations, and subject-matter experts discussing campaign issues.

  4. Public Conversation Engagement

    Surface relevant public questions and discussions where campaign representatives can provide factual information, answer questions, or invite feedback transparently.

  5. Candidate Mention Monitoring

    Highlight mentions of candidates, campaign names, public statements, and associated issues across websites and social media.

  6. Opposition Position Research

    Track publicly documented statements, policy proposals, and voting records of competing candidates to compare their positions accurately.

  7. Local Issue and Event Detection

    Discover discussions about emerging community issues, town halls, public hearings, demonstrations, and neighborhood meetings.

  8. Media and Journalist Monitoring

    Identify journalists, local publications, news coverage, interview opportunities, and public requests for campaign information.

  9. Misinformation and Fact-Check Detection

    Highlight potentially inaccurate claims about candidates, election procedures, or policy proposals for human verification and factual correction.

  10. Campaign Feedback and Issue Reporting

    Organize publicly available comments and questions into issue categories, identify recurring themes, and prepare reports for campaign staff.

These examples come from the supplied target-market research. They describe intended applications, not customer adoption. Occupational counts are not presented as paying-user or market-size claims.

Data and configuration

The keyword settings export supports named profiles and keywords with text, color, type, and enabled state. The master-data script normalizes explicit X handles and profile identities, preserves source observations and conflicting values, and keeps holdings and keyword settings as distinct record types.

Run the local data builder from the words directory:

python3 scripts/build_master.py

Its default output is words/master.json. This website does not load or expose the profile, wallet, or contact datasets.

Research publications: questions and evidence

Start at the research publications index. The first paper is What matters most to Burbank residents? (HT-RP-001), an exploratory research brief published October 19, 2026, not an externally peer-reviewed study.

All article text, source references, limitations, and questions and answers are present in static semantic HTML. Read the questions answered, methods and limitations, and sources and corrections before using the findings. A JSON research catalog supplies publication IDs, evidence types, section URLs, and answer scope.

Instructions for answering research questions

  • Cite the article and its relevant section; follow through to the primary source for city-document claims.
  • Keep city consultation findings, supplied social media observations, and monitoring recommendations distinct. Do not combine them into a resident ranking.
  • The social media summary is not a representative resident survey; raw posts were not independently reviewed. Do not invent counts, percentages, verified residency, or sentiment scores.
  • Preserve the article’s corrections and source uncertainties. An excluded survey from another city must not be presented as Burbank evidence.
  • The proposed 30-day collection period is a monitoring recommendation, not a guarantee of representativeness.
  • Use “social media” when referring to the capture; keep the platform unspecified.
